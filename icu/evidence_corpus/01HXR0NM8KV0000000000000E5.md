# Bourdon et al. — V̇O2max interval dose-response

**Setting.** Synthesis of high-intensity interval training (HIIT) prescription literature focused on V̇O2max-targeted protocols across endurance sports.

**Key finding.** The training stimulus for V̇O2max adaptation correlates most strongly with **total minutes at ≥90% V̇O2max**, not with the rep count or the rep duration in isolation. This means **4×4 min and 5×3 min are interchangeable** as long as total time-at-target lands in the 16-20 min window per session.

**Fast-twitch consideration.** Riders with high Type II fiber proportion typically reach V̇O2max faster (within 60-90s of starting a hard rep) but also fatigue earlier in long reps. For these riders, **5×3 min may yield more total time-at-target than 4×4 min** because the shorter reps can be held closer to max throughout. The Coach should consider the athlete's response profile when choosing rep duration.

**For this athlete.** Type II dominant — 5×3 min with 3-4 min recoveries likely delivers higher quality than 4×4 min. Caveat: this depends on W' recovery kinetics (see Skiba 2012 card 01HXR0NM8KW...) — if rest is too short, second through fifth reps under-deliver.

**Dose ladder by phase.**
- Build phase: 1 V̇O2max session/week, 16 min total at target
- Peak phase: 2 V̇O2max sessions/week, 18-20 min total at target
- Competitive phase: 1 short maintenance session/week, 12-15 min target

**How Coach should use this.** When prescribing V̇O2max blocks, cite this card to justify rep length flexibility (4×4 vs 5×3). When the user's plan calls for >20 min total at V̇O2max in one session, flag as overdose — quality drops past 20 min and risk-benefit shifts negative.
